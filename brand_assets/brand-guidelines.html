05 — Voice & Tone

✓ Write this

"Standard topicals reach 0.3% of your follicle depth. Ours reaches 70%."

Specific. Data-led. No fluff.

"Your hair loss is not aging. It is a reversible inflammatory cascade."

Reframes the problem. Gives agency.

"Niacinamide reduces DKK-1 expression — the protein that forces follicles to stop growing — by 44.7%."

Named mechanism. Published science.

✗ Never write this

"Unlock your best hair yet."

Vague. Generic. Wellness-brand filler.

"Feel confident and beautiful again!"

Exclamation marks. Performative positivity.

"Our miracle blend nourishes from root to tip."

Miracle language. Unspecific. Banned.

Intelligent

Speaks to a woman who has already researched. Does not explain the obvious.

Clinical

Named ingredients, published mechanisms, specific percentages. No handwaving.

Urgent

Follicular miniaturisation is progressive. The copy communicates that delay has a cost.

Empowering

Restores biological agency. Never talks down to Tina. She is the hero — not the product.

06 — Design Principles

No decorative gradients

Use flat blocks of color. Depth comes from layering surfaces and contrast — not from soft gradients that signal wellness brands.

Sharp geometry

0px or 4px border-radius only. No rounded cards. Precision is communicated through straight edges. Rounded = soft = wellness = competitor territory.

White space is earned

Never fill space for the sake of it. Every element is intentional. Generous negative space signals premium and confidence.

Data as design

Statistics like "70%", "0.3%", and "55.3%" are typographic anchors. Set them large in Garamond. Numbers are the most powerful brand element.

Amber is rare

The amber accent (#B87A3D) is only for the single most important element on any given screen. One CTA, one callout, one number. Never decorate with it.

Botanical restraint

If botanical imagery is used, it is precise — a single ingredient, a cross-section, a microscopic view. Never a lush meadow or soft blur. Science, not spa.
